# Packaging: the cover, the title, and the description

English | [中文](packaging.zh-CN.md)

Before anyone watches, reads, or scrolls into your work, they see its packaging: the cover, the title, and the first lines of the description. Together they make one promise. The content's job is to keep it. BeastCover makes the cover, but a cover only works as part of the package, so this page covers all three.

## Why packaging exists

A feed shows at least four pieces of content at once, and the viewer picks one in well under a second. That choice is not deliberate. Ask people why they clicked and they say they just felt like it. Whatever sparks more interest in that instant wins, and everything else is scrolled past no matter how good it is. A billboard on an empty highway gets read however bad it is, because there is nothing else to look at. A feed is the opposite. ([woshipm](https://www.woshipm.com/share/5978911.html))

So packaging is not decoration added at the end. Paddy Galloway, who shapes packaging for some of YouTube's largest channels, says an idea that cannot be titled and thumbnailed in a clickable way is not a good idea yet, and top creators spend about 30% of their time on ideas and packaging where small creators spend 5%. ([Colin and Samir](https://www.colinandsamir.com/resources/the-new-rules-of-youtube-from-paddy-galloway), [Creator Science](https://podcast.creatorscience.com/paddy-galloway-2/))

## One promise, three jobs

| Piece | Its job | What it carries |
| :-- | :-- | :-- |
| **Cover** | Win the glance | The tension you can see: a conflict, a before and after, the stakes, a surprising result. Words on it are a label (a number, a price, a day count), not the title |
| **Title** | Say the promise in words | What the viewer gets, stated plainly enough to search for |
| **Description** | Confirm and widen the promise | Who it is for, what they will take away, the words people search with. Only the first line or two are seen before "more" |

The three must say different things that add up to one idea. Repeating the title on the cover wastes half the space: the title is already right there next to it. Let the title carry the searchable phrase and the cover carry the tension, so together they say what neither says alone. ([vidIQ](https://vidiq.com/blog/post/youtube-thumbnail-design-tips/), [Longwave](https://www.longwave.media/blog/youtube-thumbnail-best-practices-2026))

A cover is not a poster. It does not need atmosphere, it needs to tell someone in half a second what they get by opening it. ([woshipm](https://www.woshipm.com/share/5978911.html))

## The promise has to be true

Platforms now judge the package by what happens after the click. YouTube's Test & Compare picks the winning thumbnail by share of watch time, not click rate, so a thumbnail that wins clicks and loses viewers loses the test. A high click rate with short viewing tells the platform the package lied. ([YouTube Help](https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/12340300?hl=en), [Longwave](https://www.longwave.media/blog/youtube-thumbnail-best-practices-2026))

The honest lever is the curiosity gap: show enough to make the question impossible to ignore, and leave the answer in the content. Show the before without the after. Show the result without how. Show two choices without which one wins.

## Where each piece shows

The same idea is packaged differently on each platform, because each shows the pieces differently.

| Platform | Cover | Title | Description |
| :-- | :-- | :-- | :-- |
| YouTube | 16:9 thumbnail, judged at about phone-card size | Next to the thumbnail, up to 100 characters | About 157 characters on desktop and 100 on mobile before "Show more", and roughly 138 in YouTube search ([BulkPublish](https://www.bulkpublish.com/blog/youtube-character-limit/), [touhfa](https://touhfa.art/blog/seo/youtube-description-guide/)) |
| Bilibili | 16:10 cover in a two-column feed, bottom strip covered by play counts | Under the card, best within about 12-24 characters | 250 characters in most sections ([Zhihu](https://www.zhihu.com/question/452825495)) |
| Xiaohongshu | 3:4 cover dominates the two-column feed | Under the card, 20 characters maximum | The first lines of the post body |
| WeChat articles | 2.35:1 header in the message list, the middle square in shares | Printed over the bottom of the cover | The digest, up to 120 characters, shown in share cards; left empty, the first 54 characters of the article are used ([Zhihu](https://zhuanlan.zhihu.com/p/1899150510721851888)) |
| X articles | 5:2 cover on the article card | On the card with the cover | The post that shares the article |
| Douyin, TikTok | 9:16 cover, mostly seen in the profile grid cropped to 3:4 | The caption at the bottom of the video, folded after the first lines | Same caption |

Two differences matter most. Chinese platforms accept more words on the cover than English YouTube does (Paddy notes the same for audiences across Asia), and on WeChat and X the title sits right on or next to the cover, so a quiet, editorial cover with one strong word often works better there than a loud thumbnail.

## How to package a piece

1. **Find the tension in the content.** What changes, what is at stake, what is surprising, what two things compete? If there is none, the idea needs work before the packaging does.
2. **Sketch the cover first.** One subject, one tension, at most three elements. Could someone describe it after a one-second look at phone size?
3. **Write the title** as the promise in plain words, with the phrase people would search.
4. **Write the first line of the description** as the reason to trust it: who it is for and what they walk away with. Front-load it, because the rest is folded.
5. **Read the three together.** Each should add something the others do not. Cut any word the cover already shows.
6. **Make two or three clearly different versions** of the cover when the platform lets you test (YouTube Test & Compare, Xiaohongshu's cover test), not one version in three colours.
