# What a cover is

English | [中文](what-a-cover-is.zh-CN.md)

A cover is the promise of the content, not its decoration. The cover wins the attention, the title explains the promise, and the opening keeps it. The cover and the title carry different information and only make one complete idea together, so putting the title on the cover wastes half of it. ([vidIQ](https://vidiq.com/blog/post/youtube-thumbnail-design-tips/), [Longwave](https://www.longwave.media/blog/youtube-thumbnail-best-practices-2026)) This page is about the cover. How the cover, the title, and the description split the work is in [Packaging](packaging.md).

## Why covers exist

Covers exist because there is competition. An ad in a lift gets watched however bad it is, because the people inside have nothing else to look at. A feed shows at least four covers on every screen, and the viewer holds the choice: whichever sparks more interest in that instant gets the click. The click is not deliberate. Ask people why they clicked and they say they just felt like it. ([woshipm](https://www.woshipm.com/share/5978911.html))

## What a cover has to do

In half a second, a cover has to tell someone what they get by opening it, and leave a gap they can only close by clicking. A cover is communication, not art. It is not a poster and needs no atmosphere. It needs to get the information across fast. ([woshipm](https://www.woshipm.com/share/5978911.html))

The gap is a curiosity gap: show the before and not the after, show a surprising result and not its cause. The gap has to be real, because platforms judge a cover by what happens after the click. YouTube's Test & Compare picks the winning thumbnail by share of watch time, not by click rate, so a cover that wins clicks and loses viewers loses the test. ([YouTube Help](https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/12340300?hl=en), [Longwave](https://www.longwave.media/blog/youtube-thumbnail-best-practices-2026))

## The idea beats the design

Paddy Galloway, who shapes packaging for some of YouTube's largest channels, says the idea behind a thumbnail is badly underrated: the psychology of why someone clicks matters more than colour, design, or looks, and the best thumbnail makers he knows are only average at Photoshop. ([Paddy on X](https://x.com/PaddyG96/status/1564326373170319360), [Colin and Samir](https://www.colinandsamir.com/resources/the-new-rules-of-youtube-from-paddy-galloway))

His glance test: flash the thumbnail at small size for a moment, then ask whether the viewer could tell what is in the picture.

## Culture changes the word count

Audiences in Asia accept covers with far more words than Western audiences do, which Paddy has noted too. Breakout covers on Chinese platforms are wordy and breakout YouTube thumbnails have few words, so "fewer words" is not a rule everywhere.

## Where BeastCover fits

This is why BeastCover is a set of cover types rather than one layout. Each type draws one kind of idea: a conflict (`versus`), a before and after (`before-after`), the stakes (`face-stakes`), a surprising result (`number`). Pick the type from the idea, then look at the feed preview sheet every render saves, which shows each cover at its feed size: that is the glance test.
